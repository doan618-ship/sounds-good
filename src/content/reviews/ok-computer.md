Released on the cusp of the digital millennium, Radiohead's third studio album *OK Computer* shifted the paradigm of rock music. Moving past the grunge angst of the mid-90s, Thom Yorke and producer Nigel Godrich crafted an eerie, breathtaking soundtrack for an increasingly isolated, digitized human experience.

### Soundscapes & Sonic Innovation

From the opening crunch of *"Airbag"* to the glockenspiel-accented lullaby of *"No Surprises"*, the album balances blistering electric guitars with haunting electronic textures. The multi-suite epic *"Paranoid Android"* stands as a modern-day *"Bohemian Rhapsody"*, seamlessly shifting through three distinct movements.

> "Fitter, happier, more productive. Comfortable. Not drinking too much. Regular exercise at the gym 3 days a week..."

### Cultural Impact & Legacy

Decades after its release, *OK Computer* feels startlingly predictive of modern internet culture, social fatigue, and corporate overload. Yorke's vocals soar with fragile beauty, turning existential dread into sublime art.

### Key Highlights
- **"Paranoid Android"**: A 6-minute tour de force featuring intricate guitar interplay and dramatic dynamic shifts.
- **"Karma Police"**: A dark singalong ballad anchored by melancholy piano and swirling feedback loops.
- **"Exit Music (For a Film)"**: A slow-building acoustic acoustic masterpiece culminating in thunderous bass distortion.
